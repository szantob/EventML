# EventML v0.4 — Needs — Design

**Status:** approved, ready for planning
**Date:** 2026-08-24
**Extends:** [`2026-08-09-eventml-v0.3-library-design.md`](2026-08-09-eventml-v0.3-library-design.md)
**Supersedes:** `2026-08-17-eventml-v0.4-needs-design-WIP.md`, the record of the brainstorm that ran out of
context part-way. Its §1 is carried forward here unchanged; §§2–5 were written in the resumed session, and
one of its decisions was revised. Git history holds the original.
**Origin:** the second of the three questions that opened this line of work — how continuous communication
and decision processes appear in a model. v0.2 answered accountability; this is the next piece.

---

## 1. What this release is for

The client says "I never asked for that." Today the model cannot settle the argument in either direction.
It cannot show that every request in an email produced something, and it cannot show that every line on
the quote traces back to something somebody asked for.

The release makes both checkable.

## 2. The three levels

The central result of the brainstorm. The middle level did not exist as an addressable thing before, and
its absence is why neither check was possible.

| Level | What it is | State |
|---|---|---|
| **Source** | Material of record, whole and unmodified — an email, a call note, a site visit, a tech rider, a regulation | Exists today, as a construct rather than an entity |
| **Need** | Still the stater's own words, broken into elementary units of information | **New in v0.4** |
| **Requirement** | The technical requirement derived from a need | Exists today as L1 `Requirement` |

A source is never decomposed. Granularity lives in the *reference*: a `src` names a passage within the
source. The atomising happens one level up, as `Need` entities.

**Every piece of information has a source.** A need has one, a requirement reaches one through the need it
refines, and a requirement born of professional judgement reaches a source recording that judgement. What
has no source is not information but its absence: an `unknown` value states no fact, so it has nothing to
trace.

**A need is not only the client's.** The venue states distances, an authority states prohibitions, a
performer's rider states what the act needs on stage, and our own site visit states what we measured. This
is why ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148's word is *stakeholder*, and it is what makes `Need` the right name rather than
a synonym for "client request".

### Where the levels sit

`Source` and `Need` both belong to L0, by the layer criterion the specification already uses: L0 describes
the world, L1 describes the system. A quoted measurement of a room is a statement about the world whoever
made it, so it is L0 by the same test that puts the room itself there.

Two structural consequences settle the question against placing `Need` in L1. First, `refine` stays a
boundary-crossing edge; were `Need` an L1 element, `refine` would run L1 → L1 and nothing would distinguish
it from `derive`. Second, `01-layers.md` already excludes lay narrative from L1 — "lay narrative of what
the client or audience do belongs in L0, not here" — and a verbatim quotation is lay narrative by
definition.

A tempting alternative was rejected: redrawing the L0/L1 line at *whose words these are*, with quotations
below and our own statements above. It reads well and it breaks the ranking. A shared assumption such as
"the terrace is not covered" is pointed at from below by four requirements, and `blocks` counts them;
moving it to L1 leaves four copies of one unknown and nothing to count.

## 3. The `Need` entity

```yaml
needs:
  - id: n-audience
    text: "about 300 people"
    src: { id: s-client-brief, exact: "about 300 people", start: 36, end: 52 }
    value:
      state: conflicting
      alternatives:
        - { value: 300, src: { id: s-client-brief, exact: "about 300 people", start: 36, end: 52 } }
        - { value: 450, src: { id: s-client-update, exact: "we're now looking at 450" } }

  - id: n-welcome-speech
    text: "There'll be a welcome speech around 7"
    src: { id: s-client-brief, exact: "There'll be a welcome speech around 7", start: 93, end: 130 }
```

`text` holds the stater's words, not a paraphrase. The offsets above are real, measured against the excerpt
as it stands in `examples/01-garden-party/brief.yaml`.

Where a need carries a quantity it uses the existing `state` / `alternatives` / `why` / `ask` machinery
unchanged. Where it does not — a speech, a quality expectation — it is text only.

## 4. Coverage rules

The section rests on one structural observation: **until now only one end of the `refine` edge could be
inspected.** The language already checks `satisfy` from both — rule 3 asks what was promised that nothing
delivers, rule 6 asks what is delivered that nothing promised. On `refine` that pair could not exist,
because its upper end was a path into a document rather than an element. `Need` creates it, and the two new
rules are rule 3 and rule 6 one layer up.

| | on `satisfy` | on `refine` |
|---|---|---|
| promised, nothing delivers it | rule 3 | **rule 8** — a `Need` no `Requirement` refines |
| delivered, nothing promised it | rule 6 | **rule 9** — a `Requirement` naming no origin |

**Rule 8** is the "I asked and nothing came of it" case, and it is the one new rule that can be put to a
stakeholder in their own terms: *you wrote that the restaurant serves lunch until 13:30 — is that something
we have to work around?*

For the rule to mean anything, `refines` has to hold a list. A requirement is assembled from more than one
statement: speech intelligibility answers the welcome speech and takes its headcount from the audience
count, and a single-valued edge would leave every statement that supplies a figure looking unanswered —
half the needs in any brief. Recording *which* statement was the reason and which supplied a number was
considered and rejected: that distinction is read from the requirement's own text, and reading a
requirement is work for a person or an agent, not for a script. The structure carries what a script must
check; the meaning stays in the prose where it can be judged.

**Rule 9** is the "I never asked for that" case, and one invariant defines it: **every requirement names
its origin.** It refines a `Need`, or it derives from another requirement, or it does both — the garden
party's coverage requirement is a technical consequence of speech intelligibility *and* an answer to the
client's statement about the speech. What cannot happen is neither. A requirement carrying no origin edge
at all is not a root; it is an incomplete record, and that is what the rule reports.

This is the same population an earlier draft described as "root requirements with no `refines`", stated
without needing to define a root.

Rule 9's meaning has changed from the wording the specification carries today. `03-relationships.md` now
says an absent `refines` means "the requirement did not come from the client; it came from professional
judgement", and treats that as a legitimate resting state. Under the rule that every piece of information
has a source, it is not: residual current protection comes from a wiring regulation, and a spare comes from
our own judgement, which we may record as a source from `production`. So the rule reports **a requirement
whose origin was never recorded**. It stays a question rather than becoming a modelling error, because the
language treats incompleteness as data — an unrecorded origin is a gap to close, and gaps are what the
question list exists to rank.

### Where these checks sit

The rules are not a flat list of seven, now nine. They are the checks a model is held to, and every one of
them belongs to a layer and to one of two columns: what a script can decide, and what only a person or an
agent can. The distinction matters because the second column cannot be automated later by trying harder —
it is judgement, and pretending otherwise is what produces a validator nobody trusts.

| Layer | Syntactic — a script decides | Semantic — a person or an agent decides |
|---|---|---|
| **L0** | Every `Need` anchors to exactly one passage of one source | Every relevant statement in a source has been taken into a need — the source coverage report |
| **L1** | Every requirement names its origin (rule 9); every need reaches a requirement (rule 8) | The need is correctly and sensibly turned into a technical requirement |
| **L2** | Every requirement is satisfied by something (rule 3); every logical block has a requirement behind it (rule 6) | The block actually solves the requirement it claims |
| **L3** | Every physical part is allocated (boundary rule 3) | The device chosen is the right one |

Two consequences fall out of the table, and both simplify what came before it.

**Relevance is judged once, at extraction.** A statement that will never produce a requirement should not
become a need; deciding that is the L0 semantic check, and it happens when somebody reads the source. This
is why rule 8 needs no way to mark a need as deliberately unanswered. If the rule fires, either the
requirement is missing or the need should not have been taken — and both are ordinary work.

**A failed check is a problem to solve, not a state that blocks.** The specification's older wording calls
an unallocated part "a modelling error rather than a question", which reads as though the model were
invalid. It is not: it is a model with a failed syntactic check, exactly as a brief full of unknowns is a
model with open questions. The premise in `00-overview.md` — incompleteness is data — covers both.

**Source coverage is a report, not a rule.** Which passages of a source no need cites is worth knowing and
worth reviewing, but it cannot be a question rule: every source will always contain uncited text —
greetings, "can you send a quote?" — and rule 1's own logic disqualifies a condition that never clears.
Deciding which uncited sentence was a request needs the classification D25 refuses to build, so the report
lists the uncited passages and leaves the judgement to a person, typically before a quote goes out.

**A need with no passage anchor is a modelling error**, not a question. It is the shape boundary rule 3
already has: nothing to ask, something to fix.

Ranking follows the existing definition without amendment. Rule 8 always scores `blocks: 0`, since a need
nothing refines has no requirements beneath it — the position rules 6 and 7 already occupy, and for the
reason the specification already gives. Rule 9 is the exception among the coverage rules: a root
requirement may have derived children, so it can rank above zero and reach the top of the list.

## 5. The `answers` edge

**What it records.** That one source responds to an earlier one. The edge sits on the answering source and
names the answered one, so walking backward in time is a field read — the rule every other edge in the
language follows, and the placement `supersedes` already uses on the newer `Decision`.

**Cardinality.** One `answers` edge relates exactly one answering source to exactly one answered source. A
source may carry any number of such edges in either direction, and most sources carry none. The thread —
including its branches, where two parties answer the same question — is derived from the edges; the
language names no "thread", just as it stores no requirement tree behind `derived_from`.

Stating it this way rather than as "many-to-many" is not cosmetic. An edge that is a fact in its own right
has somewhere to carry an assessment of the answer — *this reply was unusable* — which the question
lifecycle will need. A list-valued attribute has nowhere to put it.

**It is not the mail client's reply-to.** Two consequences, and together they are why the edge exists at
all. A message that is a reply but answers nothing carries no edge. An answer may arrive by a different
channel from the question — we write, the client telephones — which no mail thread can express.

**Our own question is a source.** Nothing new is needed: `from` already admits `production`, and the garden
party's site visit is such a source today. This is what lets the edge stand without the question lifecycle,
whose subject is composing the outbound letter and tracking its state.

**It changes no value.** When "Yes, plan for 450" arrives, the audience need stays `conflicting`: both
statements were made, and which one prevailed belongs to a decision, not to the statement. This is rule 4's
exception, applied to the same facts. Values move only through the existing state machinery.

**It points backward in time.** Sources carry a mandatory date, so an edge pointing forward is a modelling
error. The constraint costs nothing and rules out cycles.

## 6. What changes in the specification

Three changes are more than transcription.

**`Source` becomes an entity.** `01-layers.md` describes it today as a construct no other layer has — a
reserved key with an attribute table. Once it carries an edge and a need anchors into it, it is an entity,
because relations connect entities. This states what is already true rather than adding anything.

**A sentence leaves `06-sysml-mapping.md`,** and it is this release's clearest evidence. The `refine` row
says EventML's source is "an L0 brief path rather than a model element, **because L0 has no entities**".
The specification has been recording the defect as a note about itself; v0.4 is the release that deletes
the note.

**Version-numbered promises leave shipped prose.** Four places name v0.6 today — `02-metamodel.md:278`,
`04-uncertainty.md:310`, and `README.md` twice. Formalisation has been renumbered three times already, and
inserting the question lifecycle ahead of it would make a fourth. Shipped prose says "a later release"; the
ordering lives in one place, the versioning section of `00-overview.md`.

The rest, file by file:

| File | Change |
|---|---|
| `00-overview.md` | Two rows join the standards table: ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148 for stakeholder needs and the StRS/SyRS split, W3C Web Annotation for passage anchoring. Rule 8 requires both. The release ordering lands in §5 |
| `01-layers.md` | "Which entities may appear. Free-form structured values only" inverts: L0 has entities. The `Source` description moves to the metamodel. On the layer diagram `refines` points at `Need`, and `answers` appears inside L0 |
| `02-metamodel.md` | Eleven entities become thirteen. `Requirement.refines` changes type from an L0 path to a `Need` id. The "four usages" split is restated: `Source`, `Need` and `Decision` are alike in naming no catalogue type |
| `03-relationships.md` | Six relations become seven. `refine` reads "a stakeholder statement becomes a requirement". `trace` extends to `Need`, with passage selectors, and the file states plainly that `trace` is the one layer-independent edge — a value in `physical.yaml` cites a source in L0. The traversal walk grows from four reads to five. `derive` runs inside L1 and `answer` inside L0, a symmetry worth naming |
| `04-uncertainty.md` | Seven question rules become nine, and the rule tree gains two leaves. A table places every rule in its layer and its column — script or judgement — and the older "modelling error" wording becomes "a failed syntactic check". The ranking section states that rule 8 always scores zero and that rule 9 need not. A new subsection defines the source coverage report and why it is not a rule |
| `05-concrete-syntax.md` | Follows rather than leads: a seventh reference form for passages, a narrower role for the brief path, and the version floors |
| `06-sysml-mapping.md` | Two entity rows with no SysML counterpart, and a fifth entry under what does not map: the document-to-requirement stage SysML never sees |

| `CLAUDE.md` | The verification rule's wording — "each requirement traced back to a **brief element**" — becomes "to a `Need`". Rule 7 gains v0.4 floors. Rules 3 and 6 are untouched: needs have no catalogue type, and no new file kind appears |
| `CHANGELOG.md` | The release lands under `[Unreleased]` until it is tagged. The `[0.1.0]`, `[0.2.0]` and `[0.3.0]` sections are released history and are not rewritten |
| `docs/glossary-hu.md` | `Source`, `Need`, `answers` and *passage*. The Hungarian for `Need` needs deciding: *igény* is the obvious candidate, but the trade also says *követelmény* for it, which is the confusion D23 exists to avoid |

**No new specification file.** `07-decisions.md` earned one because it carries a recording criterion that
fits nowhere else. `Need` extends the kernel's existing structure, and every part of it belongs in an
existing section.

## 7. Verification

The examples are this language's only test, under the two rules in `CLAUDE.md` §5.

| What must be shown | Where the material already is |
|---|---|
| A need anchored by both selectors | Garden party, the two needs in §3 |
| A need with a conflicting value | Garden party's headcount, festival's cable route |
| A need from someone other than the client | Festival: the site manager and the council representative contradicting each other, and our own measurements |
| A need from a document source | Festival: the headliner's rider arrives; the other three bands' riders have not, which keeps the `unknown` that rule 1 fires on |
| `refines` naming a need | All three examples, 25 edges in total |
| `refines` naming more than one need | Garden party: speech intelligibility answers the welcome speech and takes its headcount from the audience count |
| `answers` | Garden party: record the question we sent, and "Yes, plan for 450" answers it |
| `answers` branching | Festival: one question about the cable route, answered oppositely by two parties — the branch is already in the material, unnamed |
| Rule 8 firing | Garden party: "the restaurant serves lunch until 13:30", stated and refined by nothing |
| Rule 9 firing | Conference room's `r-house-lights`. Garden party's `r-rcd-protection` shows the other half: its origin is a regulation, recorded as a source, and the rule stops firing |

Two things stay out of the examples: a need with no anchor, and an `answers` edge pointing forward in time.
Both are modelling errors, and examples show valid models — the treatment boundary rule 3 gets.

**All three examples migrate.** Per-file version floors would let an old example stay valid as v0.1, but
the release exists for two coverage checks, and neither runs on a model without needs. An example left
behind is an example the release proves nothing about.

The migration makes one existing claim vivid. Afterwards the garden party holds `"0.1"` logical and
physical files, a `"0.4"` brief, `"0.4"` requirements, and a `"0.4"` decisions file — mixed floors
demonstrated across four files rather than asserted about one.

**Offsets are measured, not estimated.** D26 takes two selectors because the quoted text is the durable
anchor and the offsets decay under editing. In the examples this bites immediately: every correction to an
`excerpt` requires re-measuring. That is part of finishing the release, not follow-up work.

**The coverage report is walked by hand,** since no validator exists. `examples/02-conference-room/README.md`
already carries this genre — it derives by hand which templates a brief triggers — so the report belongs
there, walked across one source: which sentences a need cites, which none does, and which uncited sentence
a person judged to be no request at all.

**One seam must be named.** The deferred gap values stay in the brief while everything around them moves up
into needs. A reader will take it for an oversight, so `01-layers.md` and the example README say what it is
and what will carry it away.

The release is done when all ten concepts above appear in at least one example, all three examples walk
from a device to a sentence in five reads, both new rules appear in a hand-written question list, the
coverage report is walked once, and no example contains a modelling error.

## 8. Out of scope

**The open question as a first-class concept.** Values nobody has stated — whether the terrace is covered,
how large the audience area is — are questions about the world carrying a provisional answer, and they
belong with the question lifecycle rather than here. They stay where they are, unchanged, in this release.

**Question lifecycle.** Composing the outbound letter, tracking asked / answered / unusable, and ISO 42010's
`raises` relation, deferred by v0.2's D13. Much of it becomes derivable once `answers` exists, including
the source we sent that nothing answered — but the rule that fires on it belongs to that release.

**Non-text passage anchoring.** A floor plan is a source and yields needs, but anchoring into a drawing
needs a selector the examples cannot yet exercise. W3C Web Annotation defines those selectors, so the work
is adoption rather than invention when an example calls for it.

## 9. Decisions

D22–D30 come from the first session and stand unless marked. D31 onward were taken in the resumed one.

| # | Decision | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| D22 | `Need` is a new entity in the L0 Brief layer | The middle level must be addressable for either check to run. Re-examined in the resumed session against placing it in L1, and confirmed — see §2 |
| D23 | The name comes from ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148's *stakeholder needs* | 29148 separates stakeholder needs from system requirements, and its SyRS definition — "transform the stakeholder, user-oriented view into a technical view" — is what EventML's `Requirement` already is. `StakeholderRequirement` was rejected: two "requirement" words in one language invite permanent confusion |
| D24 | The layer keeps the name `brief` | `layer: brief` sits in every model file, the spec, the glossary and three examples, and "the brief" is what a project manager says. A layer's name and its entity's name need not match — L2 is Logical and holds `Part`s |
| D25 | A source is never decomposed | Keeps L0's raw material raw, and avoids a classification taxonomy — request, fact, preference — whose boundaries would be argued forever |
| D26 | Passage references adopt the W3C Web Annotation Data Model | Both selectors, deliberately redundant: `exact` is the durable anchor, `start`/`end` stay current under editing, and when they disagree the break is visible |
| D27 | `Need.value` is optional, and the uncertainty model is untouched | A need carrying a quantity uses the existing machinery; one that carries none is text only |
| D28 | `Requirement.refines` retargets from a brief path to a `Need` id | `refines` becomes an ordinary instance reference like every other edge |
| D29 | One edge between sources: `answers` | The process, not the thread. A second edge waits for an example that needs it |
| D30 | No `topic` or grouping key on `Need` at first | If an example demands grouping, that is the argument for adding it |
| D31 | Rule 8: a `Need` no `Requirement` refines | The missing half of the `refine` pair; rule 3 one layer up |
| D32 | Rule 9 fires on a requirement carrying no origin edge at all — neither `refines` nor `derived_from` | A derived requirement inherits its origin from its parent. Stated as the invariant in D49 rather than as a bound on roots, which needed the word "root" defined |
| D33 | Source coverage is a report, not a question rule | Every source permanently contains uncited text, and a condition that never clears is noise. Separating which uncited sentence was a request needs the taxonomy D25 refuses |
| D34 | A `Need` with no passage anchor fails the L0 syntactic check | Nothing to ask, something to fix. Stated as a failed check rather than as "a modelling error": a model that fails a check is a model with work outstanding, not an invalid one, and the same rewording is due on boundary rule 3 |
| D35 | `answers` sits on the later source and names the earlier | Walking backward in time stays a field read, as `supersedes` already does |
| D36 | One `answers` edge relates exactly one source to exactly one source; a source may carry any number in either direction; the thread is derived | The multiplicity belongs at the node, not on the edge. An edge that is a fact of its own can carry an assessment of the answer later; a list-valued attribute cannot |
| D37 | `answers` changes no value | Both statements were made. Which prevailed belongs to a decision — rule 4's exception, applied to sources |
| D38 | `answers` points backward in time | Sources carry a mandatory date; a forward edge is a modelling error, and cycles are ruled out |
| D39 | No new specification file | `Need` extends the kernel's existing sections. `07-decisions.md` earned a file by carrying a criterion that fitted nowhere; this does not |
| D40 | `Source` becomes an entity | It carries an edge and is anchored into. Relations connect entities |
| D41 | Version-numbered promises leave shipped prose; the ordering lives in one place | Formalisation has been renumbered three times, and each renumbering left stale promises for review to catch |
| D42 | All three examples migrate | The coverage checks cannot run on a model without needs, so an unmigrated example proves nothing about the release |
| D43 | The coverage report is walked by hand, in the conference room README | No validator exists, and that README already carries the by-hand genre |
| D44 | Offsets are measured against the shipped text, not estimated | The redundancy in D26 is only worth having if both selectors are true |
| D45 | `Source` is material of record: a rider, a plan and a regulation are sources, and `kind` and `from` extend accordingly. Text-bearing documents ship in v0.4; drawings wait for a selector an example can exercise | A rider is a stakeholder's own statement of what the act needs — the clearest case 29148 describes. A drawing has no `excerpt`, and an unexercised construct is an unproven one |
| D46 | Rule 9 reports a requirement whose origin was never recorded, and stays a question | Every piece of information has a source, including a regulation and our own judgement. It is a gap to close, and the language treats gaps as data rather than errors |
| D47 | Values nobody has stated keep their present home; the open question becomes a first-class concept in the next release | A gap states no fact, so it has nothing to trace. Naming it properly is the question lifecycle's work |
| D48 | `refines` holds a list of `Need` ids | A requirement is assembled from more than one statement, and a single-valued edge leaves every statement that supplies a figure looking unanswered. | D50 | Checks are organised by layer, in two columns: what a script decides and what a person or an agent decides | It puts every rule, old and new, in one frame, and it names the boundary automation must not cross. Relevance is judged once, at extraction, which is why rule 8 needs no suppression flag |
| D49 | Every requirement names its origin: `refines`, `derived_from`, or both. Neither is an incomplete record | The invariant that replaces the notion of a root. It follows from the rule that every piece of information has a source, and it is what rule 9 tests |

## 10. Findings worth keeping

**SysML does not solve the passage-reference problem, because it never meets it.** Its requirements are
already model elements by the time it sees them; the document-to-requirement step happens beforehand in a
requirements tool, using ReqIF, which decomposes a document into identified `SpecObject`s. EventML models
the stage *before* that — the raw client email — so rule 8's usual list has no answer here, and W3C Web
Annotation was adopted instead.

**The brief has been asking for entities for three releases.** Its programme items already carry ids —
`b-welcome-speech`, `b-band` — and `refines` reaches them by index path anyway, `brief.program[1]`. L0
wanted addressable elements before anyone proposed them.
